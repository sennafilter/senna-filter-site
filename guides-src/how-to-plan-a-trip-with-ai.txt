Planning a trip usually means thirty open tabs and a half-finished note. AI is good at the part that takes the longest: turning your preferences into a realistic plan. It's not good at knowing today's opening hours, prices or whether a restaurant still exists. So the method is to let Claude draft and organize, and to check the facts yourself. This guide shows you how.

## New to the words?

- **Itinerary.** The plan for each day of a trip, in order.
- **Pace.** How much you do in a day. Relaxed might be two things a day. Packed might be five.
- **Anchor.** A thing you're definitely doing, like a booked tour or a flight. Everything else fits around it.

# 1. Tell Claude what kind of trip it is

~ Your preferences matter more than the destination.

```
Help me plan a trip to [place] from [dates] for [who's going]. Our budget is about [amount] per person for everything except flights. We like [things you enjoy] and don't like [things you avoid]. We want a [relaxed / medium / packed] pace. Ask me up to five questions before you suggest anything.
```

> **Start here.** Answer the questions it asks. They're usually the same ones a good travel agent would ask.

# 2. Add your anchors

~ Bookings first, then everything else.

**What it is.** Paste your flight times, hotel dates and any reservations. These can't move.

```
Here are my confirmed bookings: [paste]. Build a day-by-day plan around them. Keep arrival and departure days light. Group things that are close to each other so we're not crossing the city twice in a day. Add travel time between stops.
```

# 3. Fill the days

~ Ask for options, not one answer.

```
For each day, give me a morning, afternoon and evening plan, and one backup idea for a rainy day. Mark anything that needs a reservation or a ticket bought ahead. Tell me which things are on the plan because they're well-known and which are off the beaten path.
```

**Why it matters.** Having a backup stops one bad forecast from wrecking a day.

# 4. Check the facts

~ This step is not optional.

AI can invent or misremember details, especially for places that aren't famous. Before you rely on any plan, check these yourself on official sources:

- Opening days and hours, including holidays
- Ticket prices and whether you need to book ahead
- Whether a place is still open
- Transit routes and last trains
- Entry rules, like visas or vaccines, from the government site

```
List every fact in this itinerary that I should double-check (hours, prices, bookings, transport) and give me a checklist with what to verify for each.
```

> **The mistake.** Trusting a restaurant or hours that Claude gave from memory. Always confirm with the venue's own site or a map app before you go.

# 5. Make it usable on the trip

~ A plan you can open on your phone.

```
Turn this itinerary into a clean page I can open on my phone, with a tab for each day, the address and booking details for each stop, and a packing list based on the plans and the season. Make it a single file I can save and open without internet.
```

# 6. Plan for the unexpected

~ Five minutes now, less stress later.

Ask Claude for a short list of "if this goes wrong" notes: what to do if a flight is cancelled, where to find a pharmacy, and the local emergency number. Save it with your confirmations.

## Trip checklist

[ ] I gave Claude my preferences, budget and pace.
[ ] My bookings are the fixed points in the plan.
[ ] I checked hours, prices and bookings on official sites.
[ ] I have a rainy-day backup for each day.
[ ] I saved a copy I can open offline.
