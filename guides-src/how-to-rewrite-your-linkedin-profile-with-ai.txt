Most LinkedIn profiles read like a resume pasted into a form. Your headline is a job title, your About section is empty and nobody can tell what you're good at. A better profile answers one question for a stranger in ten seconds: what does this person do, and why would I talk to them? Claude can help you say it clearly, as long as you give it real material and keep your own voice.

## New to the words?

- **Headline.** The line under your name. It's the most-seen text on your profile.
- **About section.** A short summary of who you are and what you do.
- **Search terms.** Words people type when looking for someone like you, like "data analyst" or "nonprofit fundraising".

# 1. Decide who the profile is for

~ One reader at a time.

**What it is.** A profile for "everyone" speaks to no one. Pick the person you most want to find you: a recruiter in your field, a future client or a hiring manager.

```
I want my LinkedIn profile to attract [recruiters / clients / hiring managers] for [type of role or work]. Ask me five questions to find out what makes me a strong fit, what I'm proudest of and what I want to do next.
```

> **Start here.** Write the sentence "I help [who] do [what]." If it's hard, that's the part to work on first.

# 2. Write the headline

~ Say what you do, not just your title.

```
Here's my current role and what I do: [paste]. Write 8 LinkedIn headlines under 120 characters. Include what I do and who I help, use words people would search for, and skip buzzwords like "passionate" and "guru".
```

Pick the one that sounds like you and change a word or two.

# 3. Write the About section

~ Four short paragraphs.

```
Write my LinkedIn About section using only the facts below. Structure: one line on what I do and who for, a short paragraph on how I work, two or three specific results with numbers, and one line on what I'm looking for or how to reach me. First person, plain language, under 200 words. Facts: [paste].
```

**Why it matters.** Specific results beat adjectives. "Cut reporting time by half" beats "detail-oriented".

# 4. Tighten your experience

~ Results, not duties.

```
Here are the bullets from my last two jobs. Rewrite each to start with what I did and end with the result. Use only the facts I gave you and don't invent numbers. If a bullet has no result, ask me what happened.
```

# 5. Check it for you

~ Keep your own voice.

Read it out loud. Delete anything you'd never say in a conversation. Cut words like "leverage", "synergy" and "results-driven" and replace them with plain ones.

```
List every phrase in this profile that sounds generic or like marketing copy, and suggest a plainer way to say it that keeps the meaning.
```

> **The mistake.** Claiming something you can't back up. If a recruiter asks and you can't explain it, take it out.

# 6. Finish the details

~ Small things people notice.

- A clear, recent photo of you
- A custom profile link with your name
- Skills that match the headline, so they show up in searches
- A featured link to your best work, if you have one

```
Suggest 10 skills to list on my profile for [role], in order of how often they appear in job postings. Only include skills I actually have.
```

## Profile checklist

[ ] I know who the profile is for.
[ ] The headline says what I do and who I help.
[ ] The About section includes specific results.
[ ] No invented numbers or claims.
[ ] I removed buzzwords.
[ ] I read it out loud.
