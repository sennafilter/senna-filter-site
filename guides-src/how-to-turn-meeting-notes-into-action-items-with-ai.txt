The meeting ends, everyone nods, and a week later nobody remembers who was doing what. The fix is a short summary sent within an hour of the meeting, and most people skip it because writing it takes too long. Claude can turn your rough notes or a transcript into one in about two minutes. You still read it and send it. This guide shows the prompts and the checks.

> **Privacy first.** Don't paste confidential material into any AI tool unless your company allows it. Check your workplace's AI policy and use the tools it approves. For a fuller walkthrough, see [What Not to Paste Into ChatGPT or Claude at Work](https://www.senna-filter.com/guides/what-not-to-paste-into-chatgpt-or-claude-at-work).

## New to the words?

- **Transcript.** The word-for-word text of a meeting, from a recording tool or captions.
- **Action item.** A task with an owner and a due date.
- **Decision.** Something the group agreed on, so nobody reopens it later.

# 1. Capture something

~ Notes can be messy. Silence can't be summarized.

**What it is.** Anything works: typed bullet points, voice notes after the meeting, or a transcript if your meeting tool makes one and everyone knows it's recorded.

> **Start here.** Spend two minutes right after the meeting writing what you remember, even badly. It's better than nothing.

# 2. Extract what matters

~ Decisions, actions and open questions.

```
Here are my notes from a meeting. Pull out: 1) decisions that were made, 2) action items with the owner and due date (write "owner unclear" or "no date" if I didn't note one), 3) open questions, and 4) anything that sounds like a risk. Don't add anything that isn't in my notes.
```

**Why it matters.** The "unclear" labels are the most valuable part. They show you the gaps before they cause a problem.

# 3. Write the follow-up email

~ Short enough that people read it.

```
Write a follow-up email from me to the attendees. Start with a two-line summary, then list the decisions, then the action items as a table with owner and due date, then the open questions. Keep it under 200 words. Friendly and clear, no filler.
```

**Make it better.** Add "bold each person's name so they can find their own tasks fast."

# 4. Check it before it goes out

~ This is the step that protects you.

- Are names spelled right and owners correct?
- Do the dates match what was said?
- Did Claude include anything that wasn't discussed?
- Is anything confidential in there that shouldn't be shared with everyone?

> **The mistake.** Sending a summary that gets a decision wrong. People will treat it as the official record, so read it carefully.

# 5. Turn it into a repeatable habit

~ Same format every time.

Save the prompts above as a note you paste after every meeting, or put them in a Claude Project so you only paste the notes.

```
Every time I paste meeting notes, return: decisions, action items (owner and due date), open questions and a draft follow-up email under 200 words. Mark anything unclear.
```

# 6. Prepare for the next meeting

~ Use the last one to make the next one better.

```
Here are the action items from our last meeting. Write a short agenda for the next one that starts with a status check on each item, then covers [new topics].
```

## Meeting checklist

[ ] I'm allowed to use AI with this material.
[ ] I pulled out decisions, actions and open questions.
[ ] Every action has an owner and a date, or is marked unclear.
[ ] I checked the summary against what was actually said.
[ ] I sent it within an hour.
