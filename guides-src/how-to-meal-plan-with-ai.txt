The hardest part of dinner isn't cooking. It's deciding, at 6 p.m., with nothing planned. A meal plan fixes that, but most people quit making them because it takes an hour to find recipes and write the list. AI does both in a couple of minutes. You give it the rules of your life, and it gives you the week.

> **A note on health.** If you have allergies, a medical condition or specific dietary needs, check the plan with your doctor or dietitian. AI can get ingredients and nutrition details wrong.

## New to the words?

- **Batch cooking.** Making a big amount once and eating it across several meals.
- **Staples.** Things you almost always have, like oil, salt, rice and pasta.
- **Meal plan.** A list of what you'll eat on which days.

# 1. Tell it the rules

~ The more specific you are, the more usable the plan.

```
Plan my dinners for this week. I'm cooking [number] nights for [number] people. We like [foods] and don't eat [foods or allergies]. I have about [minutes] on weeknights and more time on [day]. My budget is about [amount] for the week. I own: [oven, slow cooker, rice cooker, air fryer, etc.]. Ask me questions if you need to.
```

> **Start here.** Be honest about your energy. A plan with a 90-minute recipe on Wednesday won't happen.

# 2. Ask for overlap

~ Using the same ingredients twice saves money and waste.

```
Make the plan so ingredients overlap. If I buy a whole rotisserie chicken, bell peppers or a head of cabbage, use it in at least two dinners. Put the quickest meals on the busiest days.
```

**Why it matters.** Half-used produce is the main way a grocery budget quietly leaks.

# 3. Get the list, sorted by aisle

~ The part that saves time in the store.

```
Turn this meal plan into one grocery list. Group it by section of the store (produce, dairy, meat, pantry, frozen). Combine duplicate items and add up the quantities. Mark anything I probably already have as "check pantry".
```

# 4. Check the details

~ Quantities and times can be off.

Look over the list for anything odd, like three cups of an ingredient the recipe uses a teaspoon of. Check cooking temperatures and times against the recipe source if you're unsure. Prices are estimates, so compare with your store's own site or app.

> **The mistake.** Following an AI recipe without reading it first. Read it once all the way through before you start cooking.

# 5. Keep what works

~ Build your own list of favorites.

After each week, tell Claude what you liked.

```
Here's what we liked and didn't like this week: [notes]. Save the winners as my "regular rotation" and use them to improve next week's plan.
```

Paste the rotation into a note, or into a Claude Project so it remembers your favorites.

# 6. Plan for leftovers and lazy nights

~ A plan needs an escape hatch.

```
Add two "emergency dinners" to my plan that use only pantry and freezer items and take 15 minutes or less.
```

## Meal plan checklist

[ ] I gave rules: time, budget, tastes and equipment.
[ ] Ingredients overlap across meals.
[ ] The grocery list is grouped by store section.
[ ] I checked allergies and quantities.
[ ] I have two emergency dinners.
